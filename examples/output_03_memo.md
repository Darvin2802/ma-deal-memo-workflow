## 8. Asset / Company Details

### A. Confirmed from transaction documents

- The assets are two existing natural gas-fired generation facilities in Texas operating in the ERCOT market.
- STEC’s indexed transaction page identifies them as:
  - Braes Bayou Power Plant in Fort Bend County, with commercial operation achieved in June 2022
  - Brotman Power Plant in Brazoria County, with commercial operation achieved in May 2023
- The same STEC source describes the portfolio as 768 MW, broken out as 384 MW for each plant.
- PROENERGY states the facilities generate 768 MW of fast-start power and use PROENERGY-manufactured equipment, including packages and PE6000 turbines.
- STEC states the assets are dispatchable, intended to provide on-demand power, and strengthen STEC’s ability to provide power during peak demand and to provide ancillary services in ERCOT’s energy-only market.
- STEC states it does not anticipate any positions to be eliminated post-closing.

### B. Supplemental from company / asset websites, filings, and other reliable sources

- PROENERGY’s historical project pages describe Braes Bayou and Brotman as fast-start peaking assets built with six LM6000 gas-turbine packages each. Those pages characterize the projects as supporting renewable integration and grid reliability in ERCOT.
- The historical Braes Bayou page says the facility began commercial operation in July 2022 in Fort Bend County and was designed as a turnkey peak-power solution that operates during peak-demand periods and extreme weather.
- The historical Brotman page says the facility began commercial operation in May 2023 in Brazoria County and supports “fast-start, reduced-emission peaking power.”

**Important source-discipline note on capacity:**  
The historical 2022 and 2023 PROENERGY project pages describe Braes Bayou and Brotman as 288 MW facilities, while the 2026 STEC transaction materials describe them as 384 MW each, totaling 768 MW. This memo uses the 2026 transaction documents for the transaction snapshot and current asset description, and flags the historical capacity discrepancy rather than resolving it without source support.

**Energy-transition relevance:**  
Although these are natural-gas assets, the retrieved sources frame them as fast-start, dispatchable resources that support renewable intermittency and reliability needs in ERCOT rather than as baseload coal or long-cycle thermal generation. That characterization comes directly from the companies’ own descriptions.

**Buyer strategic context:**  
STEC issued an RFP in July 2025 seeking up to 500 MW of firm dispatchable capacity and associated energy, including acquisition proposals for facilities in development, construction, or operation. That supports a documented buyer-side capacity procurement strategy preceding this April 2026 acquisition.
