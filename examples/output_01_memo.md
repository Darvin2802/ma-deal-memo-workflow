# Example Output 01 — GIP and EQT / AES

This is a public example output based on a real transaction clue and public-source research. It is included to demonstrate the structure and style of the workflow.

## 1. Transaction Identification

**Input headline:** GIP and EQT acquire AES

**Verified transaction title:** Consortium led by Global Infrastructure Partners and EQT agrees to acquire The AES Corporation.

**Transaction type:** Acquisition (public-company merger / take-private). The signed structure is a merger of Horizon Merger Sub, Inc. into The AES Corporation, with AES surviving as the private company.

**Verification summary:**  
The transaction is verified from primary sources. AES disclosed in its SEC Form 8-K that on March 1, 2026 it entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Horizon Parent, L.P. and Horizon Merger Sub, Inc. AES’ and EQT’s March 2, 2026 announcements describe the buyer group as a consortium led by Global Infrastructure Partners and EQT, with CalPERS and QIA as co-underwriters, acquiring AES for $15.00 per share in cash.

**Confidence in transaction identification:** High. The deal identity, structure, target, and announced consideration are confirmed by the merger announcement, SEC 8-K, and merger agreement.

**Deal value:**

- **Equity value:** $10.7 billion
- **Cash consideration:** $15.00 per share in cash
- **Equity consideration:** none for AES stockholders found in retrieved sources
- **Debt assumed:** EV includes assumption of existing debt; Exhibit 99.2 footnote says EV is based on proportional net debt of $22.724 billion as of December 31, 2025, while consolidated net debt was $27.561 billion
- **Working capital adjustment:** no confirmed working capital adjustment found in retrieved sources

- **Implied value:** no additional implied value metric beyond per-share, equity value, and EV found in retrieved sources

**% acquired / sold:** effectively all outstanding AES common shares, subject to customary exclusions for treasury, parent-held, merger-sub-held, and appraisal shares.

**Transaction structure:**  
AES entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Horizon Parent and Horizon Merger Sub. Merger Sub will merge with and into AES, and AES will survive the merger as a private company.

**Conditions precedent / approvals:**  
The deal requires AES stockholder approval, federal, state, and foreign regulatory approvals, and other customary closing conditions. The investor presentation specifically names PUCO, FERC, NYPSC, CFIUS, and certain foreign approvals. The merger agreement also refers to approvals including PUCO and NYPSC, among others.

**Board approval:**  
AES’ board unanimously approved the merger agreement and recommended that stockholders adopt it.

**Shareholder approval:**  
Required, but no completed stockholder vote was confirmed in retrieved sources.

**Financing details:**  
The consortium will fund 100% of the purchase price with equity. AES’ investor presentation states there is no financing contingency and no incremental debt will be issued as a result of the transaction. The consortium also said it intends to maintain AES’ existing capital structure and investment-grade profile.

**Termination rights / fees:**  
AES’ 8-K summarizes that Parent may owe either $100 million or approximately $588 million, depending on the termination circumstance, while AES may owe Parent approximately $321 million in certain circumstances. The merger agreement gives the exact defined amounts:
- Company Termination Fee: $320,651,487
- Parent General Termination Fee: $587,861,060
- Parent Regulatory Termination Fee: $100,000,000

**End date / outside timing:**  
The merger agreement end date is June 1, 2027, with up to two successive three-month extensions in specified circumstances.

**Competing bids / no-shop mechanics:**  
The retrieved excerpts confirm a company termination fee tied to a “Superior Company Proposal” and to a “Company Adverse Recommendation Change,” which indicates AES retained limited ability to change recommendation or accept a superior proposal under negotiated conditions. A fuller no-shop clause excerpt was not retrieved beyond those confirmed terms.

**Other material mechanics:**
- Dividends are expected to continue in the ordinary course until closing, subject to AES board approval.
- AES common stock will cease trading on NYSE after closing.
- Post-announcement, AES amended certain credit documents so that change-of-control provisions would permit direct or indirect ownership by GIM LLC, EQT Fund Management S.à r.l., QIA, and certain affiliated vehicles.

**Complex clause examples**

**Short quote:** “The Consortium will fund 100% of the purchase price to acquire the Company with equity.”

**Plain-English explanation:**  
The buyers said they are not relying on new acquisition debt at the parent level to pay stockholders. That lowers classic financing-failure risk relative to a leveraged buyout that needs large debt syndication.

**Short quote:** “Parent Regulatory Termination Fee means an amount in cash equal to $100,000,000.”

**Plain-English explanation:**  
If the deal fails because certain key regulatory approvals are not obtained under the specific circumstances set out in the merger agreement, the buyer side may owe AES $100 million. That is a targeted regulatory-risk allocation, distinct from the larger general reverse termination fee.

**Short quote:** “Parent General Termination Fee” means “$587,861,060.”

**Plain-English explanation:**  
This is the larger reverse break fee if the buyer side breaches or fails to close in the specified circumstances. It is materially larger than the regulatory-only fee, so the agreement differentiates between a regulatory failure scenario and a broader buyer failure scenario.

## 8. Asset / Company Details

### A. Confirmed from transaction documents

- AES is described in the announcement as a clean-energy and power platform across the Americas, with regulated electric utilities and competitive clean energy in the U.S. and critical energy infrastructure assets in Latin America.
- AES’ U.S. regulated utilities include AES Indiana and AES Ohio, which the announcement says will remain locally operated and managed regulated utilities after closing.
- The announcement states AES serves 1.1 million customers through those utilities.
- The announcement also states AES is the largest supplier of clean energy to corporations globally, including 11.8 GW of signed agreements to supply power to major technology firms.
- The transaction presentation says AES operates across Renewables, Utilities, and Energy Infrastructure strategic business units, and that the consortium expects to maintain those diverse operating businesses.

### B. Supplemental from company / asset websites, filings, and other reliable sources

- AES’ 2025 annual report describes AES as a global energy company “accelerating the future of energy.”
- As of 2025 year-end, AES reported total revenue of $12.233 billion, including $2.913 billion from Renewables, $4.122 billion from Utilities, and $5.402 billion from Energy Infrastructure.
- AES’ Renewables SBU had assets in operation of 17.8 GW in 2025 and total operating installed capacity of 17,836 MW across ten countries: the United States, Chile, Argentina, Colombia, Panama, the Dominican Republic, Mexico, Bulgaria, Jordan, and the Netherlands.
- AES also reported a gross global development pipeline of 41.8 GW as of December 31, 2025.
- AES’ utility footprint includes regulated operations in Indiana and Ohio. The annual report discusses AES Indiana rate-base and rate-case matters and AES Ohio regulatory proceedings before PUCO and Ohio courts.
- AES’ reported business mix makes the transaction relevant to energy-transition coverage: renewable generation, regulated grid/distribution exposure, power supply to large technology customers, and Latin American energy infrastructure are all explicitly documented in the sources retrieved.
